# Trailer narration: two scripts

Clean read-aloud text for [[STORYBOARD]]. **A** is the master: a neutral narrator, third person. **B** tells the same beats in the first person, for Diede to record later. Both fit the same scene timings, so either can be laid under the same picture.

## How to read it

- A calm, unhurried read. No trailer growl: the drama is in the picture and the music.
- About 135 words a minute. The film is about 4:46; A is about 460 words, B about 490, so about 70 percent of the film is narrated. If the first animatic feels crowded, S03b and S16b are the first lines to compress or cut.
- **The first cut uses a synthetic read of script A** (decided 2026-10-17). Script B is for Diede to record later.
- **Changed on 2026-10-17, after the pre-production checks** ([[PREPRODUCTION-CHECKS]]), in both scripts: S07 now says "marked ready" where it said "approved" (the app has no such word); S09 and S10 no longer mention readers who were left out, because the demo has none to show. The first wording of S09 was "… leaves out whoever said no, and tells you who and why." and of S10 "… and the reason anyone was left out." They do not return until unsubscription is built ([[TRAILER-BRIEF]], section 16, decision 8): Diede decided on 2026-10-17 that the film makes no claim about unsubscribing, opt-out or readers left out. S03 and S18 still say "consent" until decision 10 of the same section is made.
- **Changed on 2026-10-17, after Diede's review of the storyboard:** S03 gains the AI premise ("AI has changed what a company, or its agency, can build for itself."); a new line S03b for other systems reaching the core; S12 says "by hand or with an AI assistant"; S16 says "Email or push, any sending service"; a new line S16b says the reasons stay with the code in the same repository; S20 adds "Always adapting to your business." S08's narration is unchanged.
- Each scene is a separate take. The time in brackets is the scene's length on screen; the read should end a little before it. Leave a breath before the first word of every take; the picture starts before the voice.
- S01 and S19 have a held silence after the last word. Do not fill it.
- Numbers are spoken out ("a hundred and twenty-three"). They are the live demo's, re-read read-only from the final reset of 2026-10-17 and unchanged ([[TRAILER-BRIEF]], section 15); if the demo is ever reset again, the line is re-read.
- Captions are burned in from these texts. Keep the wording identical to what is read.

## Script A: neutral narrator, third person

**S01** [0:00 to 0:14, 20 words]
One campaign. A hundred and twenty-three readers. Three hundred and sixty-nine choices. Could you say why each one was made?

**S02** [0:14 to 0:30, 30 words]
Most companies end up choosing between two things. A tool that's easy, until they outgrow it. And a system that's powerful, until they try to change it, or leave it.

**S03** [0:30 to 0:50, 39 words]
But AI has changed what a company, or its agency, can build for itself. So what if it kept the core, its content, its campaigns, its rules, its consent, and everything around it were a part you could replace?

**S03b** [0:50 to 1:04, 26 words]
A CRM, a shop, a warehouse, an analytics tool: other systems reach the core through the API, each with its own key and its own permissions.

**S04** [1:04 to 1:14, 11 words]
This is a reference for building a communication system you control.

**S05** [1:14 to 1:22, 17 words]
A manager starts with content. Written once, kept in one place, and scored by what it's about.

**S06** [1:22 to 1:32, 19 words]
A campaign doesn't copy it; it points to it. When one headline has to change, only that email changes.

**S07** [1:32 to 1:52, 34 words]
Now one email with a slot in the middle. The slot picks for each reader, from content a person marked ready. Same email, three readers, three different picks. And every pick can be explained.

**S08** [1:52 to 2:14, 23 words]
AI can suggest and draft. A person decides what goes out. Even when a machine asks to send, someone has to say yes.

**S09** [2:14 to 2:24, 21 words]
Before anything leaves, the system plans the send, shows how many people it can reach, and freezes what they will get.

**S10** [2:24 to 2:36, 16 words]
Afterwards, open any reader's message: the content they received, and the reason each piece was picked.

**S11** [2:36 to 2:44, 15 words]
Their clicks come back as signals that fade with time, and shape the next pick.

**S12** [2:44 to 2:56, 24 words]
Behind the screens, a developer keeps it growing, by hand or with an AI assistant. Not by asking a vendor. By adding a file.

**S13** [2:56 to 3:08, 14 words]
A new content block: a manifest and a template. It appears in the editor.

**S14** [3:08 to 3:20, 16 words]
A new way to decide who sees what: one file, written by whoever knows the data.

**S15** [3:20 to 3:32, 22 words]
A new AI task. The developer defines what it may read and return; the wording of the prompt belongs to the manager.

**S16** [3:32 to 3:48, 32 words]
Even the delivery provider is a part, not a foundation. Email or push, any sending service: a file and a few lines, and the company changes who sends, without touching a campaign.

**S16b** [3:48 to 4:00, 25 words]
The reason stays with the code. Files point to the decisions behind them, including the rules for working with AI, all in the same repository.

**S17** [4:00 to 4:12, 17 words]
One campaign: an email for some readers, a push notification for others, each shaped for its channel.

**S18** [4:12 to 4:22, 18 words]
Audiences. Brands. Roles. Consent. A record of who did what. All in one place, all of it yours.

**S19** [4:22 to 4:28, 12 words]
See what it does. Change it with a file. Keep the core.

**S20** [4:28 to 4:46, 11 words]
Building a communication system you control. Always adapting to your business.

## Lines that must not be added in a re-record

Not "two files", not "a weekend", not "open source" or "free", not "GDPR", not "real email", not an open rate, not a vendor's name, nothing about unsubscribing, opt-out or readers who were left out, not "no developers needed", not "syncs with" or "connectors", not "public" documentation. The reasons are in [[TRAILER-BRIEF]], section 11.
